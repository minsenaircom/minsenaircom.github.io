How to Import Air Purifiers from China: Complete Guide 2026

Published: May 26, 2026 | by Minsen Technology

China manufactures over 80% of the world's air purifiers. For importers and distributors worldwide, sourcing from China is the most cost-effective way to build a competitive product line — but only if you know how to navigate the process correctly.

This guide walks you through every step of importing air purifiers from China, from finding the right factory to clearing customs in your home country.

Step 1: Research and Shortlist Factories

Finding the right factory is the most important decision you will make. Start with these approaches:

  • B2B platforms: Alibaba, Made-in-China, Global Sources — search for "air purifier OEM manufacturer"
  • Trade shows: Canton Fair (Guangzhou, April/October), IFA (Berlin, September), CES (Las Vegas, January)
  • Direct search: Google for "air purifier OEM factory China" — check factory websites for quality indicators

Red flags to watch for: No physical factory address listed, no export history evidence, excessively low prices, unwillingness to provide samples, no English-speaking contact person.

Green flags: Factory size over 5,000m², 10+ years in business, exports to your target market, ISO 9001 certification, own R&D team, transparent about MOQ and pricing.

Step 2: Verify the Factory

Before committing, verify the factory through multiple channels:

  • Video call tour: Ask for a live walkthrough of the production line, testing lab, and warehouse
  • Third-party audit: Services like SGS, Bureau Veritas, or TÜV can conduct factory audits
  • Existing client references: Reputable factories can provide contact information of existing clients (with permission)
  • Business license: Request a copy of the Chinese business license and export license

Step 3: Request Samples

Never place a production order without evaluating samples first:

  • Most factories offer free samples — you pay shipping (typically $50-150 via DHL/FedEx)
  • Test: CADR performance, noise levels at each speed, build quality, filter fit and seal
  • Check: Does the product match the specifications promised?
  • Consider requesting samples from 2-3 factories for comparison
  • Keep the sample as your quality benchmark for production inspection

Step 4: Negotiate Terms

Key terms to negotiate before signing a contract:

  • MOQ: Typical starting MOQ is 500 units. Some factories go as low as 100-200 for first trials.
  • Payment terms: Standard is 30% deposit, 70% before shipment (or against copy of B/L). T/T wire transfer is most common.
  • Lead time: Sample production 7-15 days, mass production 25-40 days after deposit.
  • Inspection: Negotiate the right to inspect goods before shipment (in-person or third party).
  • Warranty: Typical is 12 months from shipment date covering manufacturing defects.

Step 5: Arrange Certifications

This is where many first-time importers get stuck. Certifications must be arranged before shipping:

  • For EU: CE + RoHS + ERP (2-4 weeks testing, $2,000-4,000)
  • For Saudi Arabia: SASO/SABER (2-4 weeks, $1,500-3,000, plus annual renewal)
  • For USA: UL certification recommended (4-8 weeks, $5,000-15,000)
  • Tip: Some factories have existing certifications that cover your model. Ask about using their existing certificates with your brand name.

Step 6: Production and Quality Control

Once the deposit is paid and sample approved, production begins. Stay involved:

  • Request regular production updates with photos/videos (weekly is standard)
  • Schedule a mid-production inspection (when ~50% of units are assembled)
  • Schedule a final random inspection (AQL sampling, usually 10-20% of units checked)
  • Common QC checks: appearance, function test, noise test, packaging integrity, accessories completeness

Step 7: Shipping and Logistics

Shipping methods:

  • Sea freight (FOB): Most cost-effective for orders over 500 units. 20-35 days transit.
  • Sea freight (CIF): Factory handles shipping and insurance to your port. Same transit time.
  • Sea freight (DDP): Factory handles everything including customs clearance and duties. Best for first-timers.
  • Air freight: 5-8 days. Good for samples, small orders, or urgent replenishment. 3-5x cost of sea.

Step 8: Customs Clearance

Work with a customs broker in your country who specializes in appliance imports. Required documents typically include:

  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Certificate of Origin (most factories provide this)
  • CE/SASO/UL certificates (depending on your market)
  • Import license (requirements vary by country)

Step 9: Market Launch

With inventory in your warehouse, focus on distribution. Common channels for air purifiers include:

  • E-commerce platforms (Amazon, Shopee, Lazada, your own online store)
  • Retail partnerships (home appliance chains, department stores)
  • B2B sales (hotels, schools, offices, property developers)
  • Distributor networks (sub-distributors in different regions of your country)
