How to Choose the Right Air Purifier for Your Market: A Guide for Importers

Published: May 20, 2026 | by Minsen Technology

As an importer or distributor looking to add air purifiers to your product line, choosing the right models for your market can be the difference between a fast-selling product and dead inventory. This guide covers everything you need to know — from technical specifications to regional certifications.

1. Understanding CADR: The Most Important Number

CADR (Clean Air Delivery Rate) measures how quickly an air purifier can clean a room. It is expressed in cubic meters per hour (m³/h) or cubic feet per minute (CFM).

Quick reference guide:

  • CADR 200-300 m³/h → Rooms up to 40m² (bedrooms, small offices)
  • CADR 350-450 m³/h → Rooms up to 60m² (living rooms, open-plan spaces)
  • CADR 500-700 m³/h → Large rooms up to 80m²+ (commercial, large living areas)

Tip for importers: Always match CADR to the average room size in your target market. European homes tend to have smaller rooms than American or Middle Eastern homes, so a CADR 300 model may be sufficient for Europe while CADR 600+ is preferred for larger spaces.

2. HEPA Filter Grades: What Your Market Needs

HEPA filters are classified by their efficiency at capturing particles:

  • HEPA H10-H12: Captures ≥85%-99.5% of particles. Suitable for basic residential use.
  • HEPA H13: Captures ≥99.95% of particles. The gold standard for most markets — used in Europe, Middle East, and Asia.
  • HEPA H14: Captures ≥99.995%. Medical-grade, typically required for healthcare and commercial applications.

For most consumer markets, HEPA H13 is the sweet spot — it offers excellent filtration at a reasonable cost. If you're targeting hospitals, schools, or premium commercial clients, consider H14 models.

3. Regional Certifications: What You Must Have

Different markets require different certifications. Here is a quick overview of the most important ones for importers:

European Union (CE, RoHS, ERP):

CE marking is mandatory for all products sold in the EU. RoHS compliance covers hazardous substance restrictions. ERP (Energy-Related Products) directive applies to energy efficiency labeling.

Saudi Arabia & GCC (SASO/SABER):

SASO certification is mandatory for air purifiers entering Saudi Arabia. The SABER system requires product registration before shipment. Without SASO certification, your goods will be held at customs.

China (CCC):

China Compulsory Certification (CCC) is required for certain product categories sold in the Chinese market, including some home appliances.

Global (ISO 9001):

While not product-specific, ISO 9001:2015 certification of the manufacturing facility indicates consistent quality management and is highly valued by experienced importers.

4. OEM/ODM: Why It Matters for Importers

Working with an OEM manufacturer allows you to sell products under your own brand. Here's what to look for in an OEM partner:

  • Minimum Order Quantities: Some factories require 1,000+ units. Look for partners with flexible MOQs, especially if you are testing a new market.
  • Customization options: Can they customize housing, control panels, filters, and packaging? Full ODM capability means they can adapt existing designs to your spec.
  • Certification support: Does the factory handle SASO, CE, or other certifications, or do you need to arrange them separately?
  • Lead times: Typical OEM production takes 25-40 days. Make sure your partner's timeline aligns with your go-to-market plan.
  • Sample policy: Reputable manufacturers offer samples (often free of charge, you pay shipping) so you can verify quality before committing to a full order.

5. Choosing the Right Product Mix

Most successful importers start with a focused product range rather than trying to stock everything. Consider this approach:

  • One flagship model — High-end, feature-rich (WiFi, smart sensors, high CADR) to establish your brand.
  • One mid-range model — Best-seller for the mass market. HEPA H13, CADR 300-400.
  • One compact model — Budget-friendly entry point. Desktop or small-room purifier.

Once these sell through, you can expand your range based on real market feedback.

6. Shipping & Logistics Considerations

When importing from China, most shipments are made via sea freight (FOB Shenzhen or CIF your port). Typical timelines:

  • Sample production: 7-15 days + courier shipping (3-7 days)
  • Mass production: 25-40 days
  • Sea freight: 20-35 days to most global ports
  • Air freight: 5-8 days (for urgent or small orders)

Many OEM partners also offer DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) service, which means they handle shipping, customs clearance, and duties — making the import process much simpler for first-time buyers.
