CE vs SASO vs RoHS vs ERP: Air Purifier Certifications Explained for Global Markets

Published: May 26, 2026 | by Minsen Technology

If you are importing air purifiers and dehumidifiers from China, understanding certification requirements is critical. Shipments can be held at customs, fined, or rejected if the wrong certifications are missing. This guide explains the most common certifications for air purifiers, which markets require which ones, and how to ensure compliance.

CE Certification — European Union

Mandatory for: All EU member states, EEA (Norway, Iceland, Liechtenstein)

The CE marking indicates that a product conforms to EU health, safety, and environmental standards. For air purifiers, this typically covers:

  • Low Voltage Directive (LVD) — electrical safety
  • Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC) — no interference with other devices
  • Restriction of Hazardous Substances (RoHS) — material composition

Without CE marking, your products cannot be legally sold in any EU country. The manufacturer must issue a Declaration of Conformity and affix the CE logo visibly on the product.

SASO/SABER Certification — Saudi Arabia & GCC

Mandatory for: Saudi Arabia, recognized by other GCC states

SASO certification is one of the most important certifications for air purifier exporters targeting the Middle East. The SABER online system requires:

  • PCoC (Product Certificate of Conformity) — obtained before shipment, valid 1 year
  • SCoC (Shipment Certificate) — per-shipment certificate for customs clearance

Key requirements for SASO compliance: IEC 60335 safety testing, 220-240V/50Hz configuration, Arabic labeling on packaging and manuals, and energy efficiency registration.

RoHS — Restriction of Hazardous Substances

Required for: EU, also referenced by many other markets

RoHS limits the use of specific hazardous materials including lead, mercury, cadmium, hexavalent chromium, PBB, and PBDE. Most reputable manufacturers use RoHS-compliant components as standard. RoHS compliance is often rolled into the CE certification process.

ERP — Energy-Related Products Directive

Required for: European Union

ERP sets energy efficiency requirements for products sold in the EU. Air purifiers must meet minimum efficiency standards and carry an energy label showing their efficiency class (A-G). ERP compliance affects product design — particularly motor efficiency, standby power consumption, and filter replacement indicators.

CCC — China Compulsory Certification

Required for: Products sold within the Chinese domestic market

CCC is mandatory for certain product categories sold in China. For air purifiers, CCC covers safety and performance requirements. If you plan to manufacture in China but sell internationally (not domestically), you typically do not need CCC.

UL/ETL — North America

Required for: USA and Canada (voluntary but market-expected)

While UL certification is not legally mandatory in the US, most major retailers require it. UL standards for air purifiers cover electrical safety, fire risk, and ozone emissions. Products for North America must be configured for 110-120V/60Hz.

ISO 9001:2015 — Quality Management

Applies to: The factory, not the product

ISO 9001 certification of the manufacturing facility indicates consistent quality management. While not product-specific, it is a strong signal to importers that the factory follows standardized processes for quality control, traceability, and continuous improvement.

How an OEM Factory Can Help with Certifications

A good OEM manufacturer does more than just build products. They can:

  • Pre-configure products for your target market's voltage and plug type
  • Provide test reports and technical files needed for certification applications
  • Maintain current certifications for popular models (reducing your time to market)
  • Advise on market-specific requirements you might not know about
  • Connect you with approved testing labs and certification bodies
