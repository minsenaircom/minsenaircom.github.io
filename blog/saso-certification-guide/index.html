SASO/SABER Certification for Air Purifiers: Complete Guide for Saudi Arabia Importers

Published: May 22, 2026 | by MINSEN

Saudi Arabia is one of the largest import markets for air purifiers and dehumidifiers in the Middle East, driven by high dust levels, extreme summer heat, and growing awareness of indoor air quality. However, getting your products into the Saudi market requires navigating the SASO/SABER certification system — a process that trips up many first-time importers.

This guide covers the main SASO/SABER considerations for air purifiers and dehumidifiers. Final requirements must be confirmed for the exact product, importer and current Saudi regulation.

What Are SASO and SABER?

SASO (Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization) is the national standards body of Saudi Arabia. Products must be tested to SASO standards before they can be legally imported and sold.

SABER is the electronic online platform launched in 2019 for product certification. All regulated products must be registered in the SABER system to obtain a Product Certificate of Conformity (PCoC) before shipment, and a Shipment Certificate (SCoC) at the time of import.

Think of it this way: SASO sets the technical standards your product must meet, while SABER is the online system where you register and prove compliance.

Do Air Purifiers and Dehumidifiers Need SASO Certification?

Yes, absolutely. Air purifiers and dehumidifiers fall under the "Technical Regulations for Electrical Appliances" in Saudi Arabia. Products in this category require:

  • IEC 60335-1 / IEC 60335-2-65 — Safety testing for air cleaning appliances
  • SASO 2897 or equivalent — Energy efficiency requirements
  • Low Voltage Directive compliance — Electrical safety (220-240V, 50Hz for Saudi)
  • EMC Directive compliance — Electromagnetic compatibility
  • Registration in the SABER platform

Step-by-Step SASO/SABER Certification Process

Step 1: Product Testing

Your product must be tested by an ISO 17025 accredited laboratory to verify compliance with relevant standards. Tests typically include:

  • Electrical safety tests (ground continuity, insulation, leakage current)
  • Performance testing (CADR measurement, noise levels)
  • Material flammability testing
  • EMC testing

Timeline: 2-4 weeks depending on the lab and product complexity.

Step 2: Obtain a Certificate of Conformity

After successful testing, a Notified Body (such as TÜV, SGS, Intertek, or Bureau Veritas) will issue a Certificate of Conformity. This certificate is valid for 1-3 years depending on the product category.

Step 3: Register in the SABER Platform

Log in to the SABER online platform and register your product. You will need:

  • Your Certificate of Conformity
  • Product technical file
  • Manufacturer declaration
  • Importer/brand registration

Once approved, you receive a Product Certificate of Conformity (PCoC) — valid for one year and required before shipping.

Step 4: Shipment Certificate (SCoC)

For each shipment, you must apply for a Shipment Certificate through SABER. This involves verifying that the shipped products match the registered product specifications. The SCoC is issued for each specific shipment and is required for customs clearance.

Step 5: Customs Clearance

Your goods arrive at a Saudi port (usually Jeddah or Dammam). Customs will check the SCoC and may inspect goods. With proper certification, clearance typically takes 2-5 days.

Common Mistakes Importers Make

  1. Assuming CE = SASO: CE certification does not automatically qualify your products for Saudi Arabia. SASO testing is separate and must be done specifically for Saudi standards.
  2. Voltage mismatch: Saudi Arabia uses 220-240V, 50Hz. Products configured for 110V (USA/Japan) or 60Hz only will not pass certification.
  3. Not registering in SABER before shipping: You must obtain the PCoC before the goods leave China. Trying to register after shipping causes major delays.
  4. Incorrect HS Code classification: Air purifiers and dehumidifiers fall under different HS codes. Incorrect classification can lead to customs rejection.
  5. Ignoring Arabic labeling requirements: Saudi regulations require instructions and warning labels in Arabic.

How an OEM Partner Can Help

An experienced sourcing partner can help organize the information required by the SASO/SABER process:

  • Confirm which supplier tests and documents are available for the proposed model
  • Request missing technical information before the sample or production stage
  • Check certificate holder, model number, standards and validity instead of assuming an existing file covers the product
  • Coordinate the required electrical configuration and Arabic labelling requirements
  • Align document requests with your Saudi importer, agent or conformity-assessment provider

Model-specific certification status is confirmed during project review. A certificate shown for one version of a product should not be treated as proof for every similar model.

Conclusion

SASO/SABER requirements must be reviewed for the exact product entering Saudi Arabia. Confirm the current scope with the manufacturer, your importer and the relevant conformity-assessment provider before shipment.
