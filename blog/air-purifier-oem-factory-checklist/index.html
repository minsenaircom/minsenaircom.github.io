Air Purifier OEM Factory Checklist: What to Look for in a Chinese Manufacturer

Published: 2026-02-10

How to Choose an Air Purifier OEM Factory in China

Selecting the right OEM partner is one of the most important decisions you'll make for your air quality brand. With hundreds of factories in Guangdong alone, here's a practical checklist to help you evaluate potential partners.

1. Verify Certifications

A legitimate air purifier OEM factory should have:

  • ISO 9001:2015 — Quality management system certification
  • CE certification — For European market access
  • RoHS compliance — Restriction of hazardous substances
  • SASO/SABER — For Middle East market access (Saudi Arabia)
  • CCC certification — China Compulsory Certification (indicates legitimate factory)

Ask: Can you share your certification documents and test reports?

2. Evaluate R&D Capability

A strong R&D team means better product customization and faster time to market.

  • How many engineers are on the R&D team?
  • Do they use CAD/CAM software for custom design work?
  • Can they handle both hardware and software (WiFi/app) development?
  • How quickly can they produce samples for new designs?

Development time and engineering support belong to the selected manufacturing partner. Ask for the current sample plan, engineer contact and written scope before relying on any timing claim.

3. Inspect the Factory

If possible, visit the factory or request a virtual tour:

  • Production lines: How many? What's the monthly capacity?
  • Testing lab: Is there an on-site lab for CADR, noise, and safety testing?
  • Warehouse: Is there adequate space for raw materials and finished goods?
  • Cleanliness: A clean factory indicates quality management

4. Understand Product Range

Partners with broader product lines offer more flexibility:

  • Air purifiers at different CADR levels
  • Dehumidifiers (compressor and desiccant types)
  • Complementary products (ice makers, heaters, humidifiers)
  • Component standardization across models (reduces your spare parts inventory)

5. Check Quality Control Processes

Ask about specific QC procedures:

  • What percentage of units are tested before shipping?
  • What tests are performed (CADR, noise, safety, burn-in)?
  • How are defects handled?
  • What is the warranty policy?

Do not accept a general quality statement. Ask for the actual inspection plan, sample records and test standards used for the proposed model.

6. Communication and Support

Responsive communication is crucial for OEM partnerships:

  • Do they have English-speaking sales and technical staff?
  • How quickly do they respond to inquiries (aim for within 24 hours)?
  • Do they provide regular production updates?
  • What after-sales support do they offer?
